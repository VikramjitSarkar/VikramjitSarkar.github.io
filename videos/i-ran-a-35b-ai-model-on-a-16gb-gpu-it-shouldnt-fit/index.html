I Ran a 35B AI Model on a 16GB GPU. It Shouldn't Fit.

27 October 2026 · 4:46 · 14 views · by Vikramjit Sarkar

Watch on YouTubeSubscribe for the next test

Is 16GB VRAM enough for local AI? I ran Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B, a 35B model that is bigger than the card, on an RTX 4080 SUPER 16GB with llama.cpp, and it still wrote at ~80 tokens per second. Here's why it works, the hidden number nobody benchmarks, and the 4 settings to copy.

0:00 A 35B model on a 16GB GPU

A 35 billion parameter AI model. A graphics card with 16 gigabytes. On paper, it doesn't fit. The model file alone is bigger than the card. And yet, it wrote at 80 tokens a second. Faster than you can read. So is 16 gigs enough, or is it a trap? Stick around, because the number that really decides it isn't the one on the box. It's one nobody puts in their benchmarks, and I'll show you mine.

This is Upgrade with V.

0:33 The question everyone skips

Every week, a video says 16 gigabytes is all you need. They're not lying. But they only answer one question. Does the model load? The question that matters is: once it loads, is there room left to actually work? Quick one before we dig in. Most people watching this aren't subscribed. It's free, it's one click, and unlike your graphics card, it never runs out of memory.

1:01 What fills your VRAM (weights vs KV cache)

Your graphics card holds two things. The first is the weights. The model itself. That size is fixed. The second is the KV cache. That's the conversation. Every word you send, and every word it writes back, leaves a small footprint, so the model doesn't have to recompute it. And that grows. For a typical 32B model, it's about a quarter of a megabyte for every single token. 8,000 tokens, 2 gigabytes.

32,000, 8 gigabytes. A dense 32B at 4-bit is already about 20 gigabytes on its own. On a 16 gig card, that's game over before you type a word. But here's the twist. My card broke that rule, and the model was even bigger.

1:48 How mixture of experts makes it fit

So how did a 35B model run on my 16 gig card? Because it isn't one giant brain. It's a mixture of experts. 35 billion parameters in total, but only about 3 billion do the work for each word. So llama.cpp keeps the parts that run on every word on the graphics card, and parks most of the experts in normal system RAM. My numbers: 15.7 of 16 gigabytes used on the card, and about 14.5 gigabytes of experts sitting in RAM.

A normal dense model spilling into RAM would crawl. Experts in RAM barely hurt, because each word only visits a few of them. If that just saved you from buying a bigger graphics card, hit like. It costs 0 gigabytes.

2:35 The hidden number: reading speed

Now, the number nobody shows you. That tokens per second counter? It only measures writing. Before the first word appears, the model has to read your whole prompt. Here's what that cost on my card. A 2,000 token prompt: first word in 1.1 seconds. 8,000: 4.5 seconds. 21,000: 10 seconds. Writing speed barely moved, from 80 to 77. But the wait before the first word grew 9 times. In a chat, you'll never notice.

In a coding agent that resends huge prompts every turn, this wait is your real speed. So check that your server reuses its cache, instead of rereading everything.

3:22 4 llama.cpp settings to copy

4 settings that made this work in llama.cpp. 1. Pick a mixture of experts model, not the biggest dense model that loads. 2. Move the experts to RAM with the CPU-MoE option, and keep everything else on the card. 3. Set your context length on purpose. Don't leave it at the maximum. 4. Store the KV cache in 8-bit instead of 16. It roughly halves that memory, with almost no quality loss.

Know someone about to spend $2,000 on a new graphics card? Send them this first. Their wallet will thank you.

4:02 Verdict: is 16GB enough?

So, is 16 gigabytes enough? For a mixture of experts model, yes. Comfortably. 80 tokens a second, drawing about 150 watts. For a big dense model with a long context, no. Size the model for the conversation, not the conversation for the model. Every number in this video came from my own PC. No sponsors, no screenshots from strangers.
